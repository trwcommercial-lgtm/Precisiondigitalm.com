The real example

A monthly route
instead of a phone call.

Most snow removal waits for the phone to ring. Barret4Seasons sold a spot on his route instead. Members pay every month whether it snows or not, and he shows up during or shortly after a snowfall.

That is a real offer. Someone scrolling Facebook in November understands it in four seconds. It's why $275 was enough. Read the full Elk Grove Village case study.

How Meta works now

The ad picks
the audience.

Meta reads your ad and your landing page and decides who sees them. Interest targeting matters less every year. If your creative speaks to homeowners with a driveway, those are the people Meta finds.

When an account underperforms, I look in three places, in this order. The signal, meaning what counts as a conversion. The structure, meaning budget spread too thin. Then the creative, meaning ads pulling the wrong people.

Tracking

Fix the signal
first.

The Pixel alone isn't enough. We connect the Conversions API as well, so both send your lead event. Then we submit a test lead and confirm it shows up exactly once.

Next I compare Meta's 30-day lead count against your own records. Far apart means every other number in the account is wrong, and that gap is the first thing I show you.

After the click

Leads go cold
in minutes.

A cheap lead nobody calls costs more than an expensive one who books. On one Chicago suburbs gym campaign, Meta ads fed an automated booking agent that put interested people straight on the schedule, and the club paid only for verified walk-ins. Monthly personal training revenue went from about $12,500 to $34,000 while it ran. Details are on the Results page.

That follow-up is what a booking system does. It answers while the person still cares.
